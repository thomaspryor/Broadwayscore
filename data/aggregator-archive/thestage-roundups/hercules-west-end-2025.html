Hercules at the Theatre Royal Drury Lane - review round-up

Review Round-UpsJun 27, 2025
Luke Brady in Hercules at the Theatre Royal Drury Lane, London. Photo: Matt Crockett/Disney

For 30 years, Disney has been slowly shepherding its catalogue of classic movies from screen to stage. One – the long-running Lion King – has been phenomenally successful. Others – Tarzan, Aladdin, Frozen – have had mixed fortunes.

Next up is Hercules. Based on the 1997 animation of the same name, this musical version premiered in New York City’s open-air Delacorte Theater in 2019. Reworked iterations subsequently ran in New Jersey and Hamburg. Now, it arrives in the West End.

With a book by Tony Award-winning playwright Robert Horn and former Young Vic boss Kwame Kwei-Armah, a score by EGOT Alan Menken and David Zippel, and direction from Broadway bigshot Casey Nicholaw, Hercules stars Luke Brady as the titular Greek hero and is in residence at the Theatre Royal Drury Lane for the foreseeable future.

Does this heroic team successfully lift Hercules from screen to stage? Do the reviewers rave about this mythological musical? Does Disney have another hit on its hands?

Fergus Morgan rounds up the reviews...

Hercules review
Casey Nicholaw

Hercules – a mythological mess?

Classical scholars beware: Hercules plays fast and loose with the ancient myth, aping the film in following the eponymous demigod on an adolescent quest to reclaim his divine birthright, after he is ejected from Mount Olympus by the dastardly Hades.

A couple of critics are entertained. Olivia Rook (LondonTheatre, ★★★★) enjoys “big tunes, gorgeous costumes and plenty of groan-worthy laughs”, while Claire Allfree (Telegraph, ★★★★) admires a “fleet-footed” adaptation that “has retained the original’s goofy knockabout humour”. For anyone under 10, she reckons, Hercules is “a blast”.

Most, though, are not impressed. The show is “laboured” for Patrick Marmion (Daily Mail, ★★), “heavy-handed” for Sarah Crompton (WhatsOnStage, ★★★) and “underwhelming” for Clive Davis (Times, ★★★), while Fiona Mountford (iNews, ★★) finds it “a hideous grab-bag of classical mythology” that unfolds over “two charm-free hours”, Arifa Akbar (Guardian, ★★★) labels it “a conveyor-belt musical” that is “not so much divine as 2D” and Andrzej Lukowski (TimeOut, ★★) calls it simply the next “unit” in “the megacorp’s long line of perfectly adequate, not very imaginative adaptations”.

“Disney made Hercules in its boldest era, the 1990s,” writes Alice Saville (Independent, ★★). “The film’s gloriously left-field take on Ancient Greece converted the muses into a gospel choir, sketched its characters with the spiky clarity of amphora paintings, and turned this hero’s rise into a pacy satire of celebrity culture. Unfortunately, there’s none of the same imaginative energy in this stodgy new stage version.”

The Muses are ‘a whole lot of finger-snapping, head-shaking, quick-changing fun’. Photo: Johan Persson/Disney

Hercules – so-so score, disappointing design

As drama, then, Hercules generally disappoints. Does it serve up more musically, though? Composer Menken has won a heap of awards for his work in film and theatre over the last fifty years. Here, he reunites with lyricist Zippel, with whom he created the soundtrack for the original 1997 animation. Are the critics captivated?

Nope. "Menken and Zippel’s score includes hits from the original film and some new numbers added for the 2019 off Broadway premiere,” explains Nick Curtis (Standard, ★★★), but “none lingered in the ear or the mind on the journey home.” Others agree. The show’s music is “generic” for David Benedict (Variety), “efficient but unmemorable” for Davis, and “samey and a little soupy” according to Akbar. On top of that, adds Sarah Hemming (Financial Times, ★★★), “the sound design makes it hard to hear the lyrics.”

So much for the songs: what about the staging? Disney stage shows are famous for being visually spectacular. Do director Nicholaw, set designer Dane Laffrey, and costume designers Gregg Barnes and Sky Switser deliver something dazzling here?

The costumes are impressive – a “heavenly” and “camp as hell” mix of “gold dresses” and “white Spanx” with “Hercules in a mesh vest and miniskirt-style toga”, describes Akbar – but the rest of the production largely disappoints. The choreography is “zestless” for Marmion and “lacklustre” for Benedict, while Lukowski likens the show’s video-splashed design to “a themed restaurant” and Davis adds that it “looks more like a Vegas theme park on a quiet Sunday afternoon” than a West End show.
Continues...

Mae Ann Jorolan as love interest Meg is ‘enjoyably feisty’. Photo: Johan Persson

Hercules – boyish Brady, magnificent muses

Since graduating from RADA in 2016, actor Brady has assembled an impressive CV, with credits at London’s Young Vic, the Chichester Festival Theatre, Shakespeare’s Globe, the National Theatre and the Royal Shakespeare Company. In 2020, he starred as Moses in the West End version of The Prince Of Egypt. Here, he takes on another ancient character.

Most critics are impressed by his Hercules: he is “boyish and likeable” according to Lukowski, “camp and joyful” according to Saville, and “brings the right combination of beefcake and boy-next-door innocence and charm” according to Sam Marlowe (The Stage, ★★★). And he sings with “a voice of molten sugar”, adds Marmion.

Elsewhere, there is praise for Mae Ann Jorolan as love interest Meg – she is “enjoyably feisty” for Marlowe – but Stephen Carlile’s Hades splits the critics. He is “deliciously enjoyable” for Benedict, but strangely “telly host-esque” for Saville. This Hades “feels as though he’s trading kitchen gadgets on QVC, not tormented human souls”, she notes.

The most praise is reserved for the five muses, played by Malinda Parris, Candace Furbert, Robyn Rose-Li, Sharlene Hector and Brianna Ogunbawo. They “combine magnificent gospel-riffed singing with plenty of humour and knowing sass” in “an ever-changing wardrobe of outrageous, shiny costumes”, describes Crompton, while Lukowski labels them “a whole lot of finger-snapping, head-shaking, quick-changing fun” and Stefan Kyriazis (Express, ★★★) admires how these “five swaggering, powerhouse divas bring the entire auditorium alive”.

Hercules – the verdict

The auguries are not encouraging for Disney’s latest venture into stage shows. This adaptation of The Mouse House’s 1997 mythological movie earns a couple of positive reviews, but most critics cannot find much to admire. Brady provides a strong performance as the titular hero, and is well supported by the five muses, but the story is unimaginative, the score disappoints, and the staging lacks style and spectacle.

Four-star reviews from The Telegraph and LondonTheatre are as good as it gets. The rest of the critics award only two or three stars. It might satisfy children over the summer, but the task of turning Hercules into a hit musical, it seems, has proved too tricky here.
