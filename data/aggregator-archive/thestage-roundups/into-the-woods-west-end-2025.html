Into the Woods at the Bridge Theatre – review round-up

Review Round-UpsDec 15, 2025
Gracie McGonigal in Into the Woods at Bridge Theatre, London. Photo: Johan Persson

In 1986, Stephen Sondheim’s Into The Woods opened at the Old Globe Theatre in San Diego, arriving on Broadway the following year. Now, nearly 40 years later, and four years since the great composer/lyricist died, the musical is receiving a major revival at London’s Bridge Theatre.

The second collaboration between Sondheim and book writer James Lapine, after 1984’s Sunday in the Park with George, Into the Woods freely intertwines the plots of several Brothers Grimm fairytales – Little Red Riding Hood, Jack and the Beanstalk, Cinderella and Rapunzel – with the story of a baker and his wife, cursed to be childless by a witch.

Directed by Jordan Fein, whose 2024 production of Fiddler on the Roof earned a whopping 13 Olivier Award nominations, and designed by Tom Scutt, this revival stars Jamie Parker, Katie Brayben, Kate Fleetwood, Michael Gould, Oliver Savile and Chumisa Dornford-May, running at Nicholas Hytner and Nick Starr’s venue until at least April.

Does Sondheim’s fairytale fantasia find favour with the critics once again? Do the reviewers rave about another revival from Fein? If you go Into the Woods today, are you in for a festive treat?
Fergus Morgan rounds up the reviews...

Into the Woods review
Stephen Sondheim

Into the Woods – a riveting revival

Into the Woods was first seen in the West End in 1990, and revived at the Donmar Warehouse in 1998. Fein’s take is “captivating” for Clive Davis (Times, ★★★★★), “magical” for Sarah Crompton (WhatsOnStage, ★★★★★), “breathtaking” for Louis Chilton (Independent, ★★★★) and “spellbinding” for Marianka Swain (LondonTheatre, ★★★★★). According to Andrzej Lukowski (Time Out, ★★★★★), it is “a sublime production of a sublime musical”.

Into the Woods is “the work of a genius at the peak of his powers: a clever send-up of fairytales that pushes familiar stories into absurd, existential, eventually very moving territory”, continues Lukowski. “It’s both playful and profound, mischievous and sincere, cleverly meta but also a ripping yarn.” And “rising star” Fein “smashes it” with a production of “pure magic”.

Not everyone agrees. Sam Marlowe (The Stage, ★★★) reckons that the show “looks and sounds sumptuous” but finds it lacking in “any fresh or surprising flavour”. Most, though, concur with Nick Curtis (Standard, ★★★★). Fein, following Fiddler on the Roof with this “triumphant” production, is “proving himself a revelatory reviver of musicals”, he concludes.

Chumisa Dornford-May. Photo: Johan Persson

Into the Woods – a sensationally sylvan staging

Scutt has worked on some of the biggest productions of the last decade, including King Charles III, Jesus Christ Superstar, A Very Expensive Poison, Cabaret at the Kit Kat Club, and Fein’s acclaimed revival of Fiddler on the Roof. What does the designer deliver here?

His design is “exquisitely conceived”, writes Arifa Akbar (Guardian, ★★★★). “The pitch-black backdrop is a dramatic canvas. It cracks open to a woodland with birdsong and shafts of light to become a living picture-book of arboreal splendour, but with all of the Grimm’s gothicism.”

The set is “jaw-dropping”, according to Swain, “astonishing”, according to Lukowski, and “beautiful”, according to Dominic Cavendish (Telegraph, ★★★★). “Scutt costumes everyone deliciously, down to the smallest detail,” adds Marlowe. The show is “storybook perfection.”

Sondheim’s “lush, motif-saturated score of baroque nursery rhymes feels as vividly alive as the forest,” adds Lukowski, while Davis calls Mark Aspinall’s 12-piece orchestra “elegance itself” and Crompton praises both Jonathan Tunick’s orchestrations and Adam Fisher’s “terrifyingly good sound design”. The giant, she notes, is realised in “absolutely convincing” fashion.

Katie Brayben in Into the Woods at the Bridge Theatre, London. Photo: Johan Persson
Oliver Savile and Hughie O'Donnell. Photo: Johan Persson

Into the Woods – an extraordinary ensemble

Plenty of famous names have featured in previous productions of Into the Woods, and this revival is no different, featuring Parker and Brayben as the baker and his wife, Fleetwood as the Witch, and Gould as the Narrator.

It is Fleetwood’s performance that earns the most acclaim. She is “sensational”, according to Cavendish, brings “complexity, heart, and a comic range equal to her vocal one”, according to Lukowski, and displays “immense wit and pathos” as “a roguish, snaggle-toothed, nightmare horror, with her leather corset, twisted body and popping eyes”, according to Marlowe.

Fleetwood does “career-best work as the Witch”, adds Swain. “She brings everything from withering disdain and panto-villain glee as she bullies these tiresome mortals” to "tender desolation in her exquisitely sung maternal lament”. It is “a knockout performance”.

Dornford-May makes for “an extraordinarily clear and charismatic Cinderella”, Oliver Savile has “knowing panache” as both Cinderella’s Prince and the Wolf who hunts Gracie McGonigal’s “bolshie” Red Ridinghood, and Parker and Brayben “bring a truth and sincerity to every note they sing and every line they say”, describes Crompton. “There isn’t a weak link among them.”

Into the Woods – the verdict

The first musical that the Bridge Theatre staged, Guys and Dolls, was a huge hit. This, the venue’s second, is similarly showered with praise. Thirty years since Sondheim’s fairytale favourite premiered, and four since the great composer died, Jordan Fein has followed up his celebrated staging of Fiddler on the Roof with an extraordinary Into the Woods, featuring a sumptuous set from designer Tom Scutt and a cracking performance from Kate Fleetwood.

Five-star reviews in the Times, WhatsOnStage, LondonTheatre and Time Out, plus four-star write-ups from most other critics suggest that this is a late contender for show of the year.
