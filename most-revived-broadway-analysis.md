### 2 Broadway Revivals (selected)

Annie, Anything Goes, Camelot, Carousel, Company, Follies, Grease, How to Succeed in Business Without Really Trying, Into the Woods, Kiss Me Kate, La Cage aux Folles, Les Miserables, Once Upon a Mattress, Ragtime, She Loves Me, Show Boat, Sunday in the Park with George, Sunset Boulevard, Sweet Charity, The Music Man, The Pajama Game, The Pirates of Penzance, The Rocky Horror Show, The Wiz, and more.

### Patterns

- **Rodgers & Hammerstein dominate the top tier.** Oklahoma!, Carousel, The King and I = 15 combined revivals. The Golden Age of musicals keeps getting revived because these scores are bulletproof.
- **Sondheim is the modern revival king.** Sweeney Todd, Company, Into the Woods, Follies, Sunday in the Park with George -- 5 titles with multiple Broadway revivals, more than any other post-1960 composer.
- **The 2020s star-revival boom.** Gypsy (Audra McDonald), Cabaret (Eddie Redmayne), Sunset Boulevard (Nicole Scherzinger), Sweeney Todd (Josh Groban), Into the Woods -- star-driven revivals are peaking. The trend: a major name attached to a beloved title is the surest Broadway investment.
- **Contrast with plays.** The Playbill play list is dominated by three writers: Arthur Miller, Tennessee Williams, and Eugene O'Neill. Musical revivals spread across far more composers -- no single writer has 3 titles at 5+ revivals.
- **Gypsy is the ultimate "star vehicle" musical.** Every revival is defined by its Rose: Angela Lansbury (1974), Tyne Daly (1989), Bernadette Peters (2003), Patti LuPone (2008), Audra McDonald (2024). No other musical has been revived this consistently as a showcase for one role.

**19 different Shakespeare plays, ~60+ total productions since 1945.**

### Notable Productions

- **The Burton Hamlet (1964)** -- Directed by John Gielgud at the Lunt-Fontanne, Richard Burton's Hamlet ran 137 performances and remains one of the most legendary Broadway productions of any kind.
- **Paul Robeson's Othello (1943)** -- 296 performances, still the longest-running Shakespeare production in Broadway history. The first African-American actor to play Othello on Broadway.
- **Daniel Craig's Macbeth (2022)** -- The most recent Broadway Shakespeare, at the Lyceum.
- **Kit Connor & Rachel Zegler's Romeo and Juliet (2024)** -- Proved the Bard can still sell on Broadway with the right casting.
- **Glenda Jackson's King Lear (2019)** -- Gender-blind casting of the title role, with a Philip Glass score.

### Patterns

- **Hamlet actually edges out Macbeth** (13 vs 12) for most-produced Shakespeare on Broadway since 1945 -- though Macbeth has been more consistent in recent decades.
- **Shakespeare on Broadway is in steep decline.** 30+ productions in 1945-1969 vs. roughly 6 in the entire 2020s. The economics of Broadway houses make straight Shakespeare increasingly hard to justify -- a $200 ticket needs spectacle.
- **The star vehicle era.** Modern Broadway Shakespeare lives or dies by its lead: Daniel Craig (Macbeth), Jude Law (Hamlet), Kit Connor & Rachel Zegler (Romeo and Juliet), Patrick Stewart (Macbeth), Ralph Fiennes (Hamlet), Glenda Jackson (King Lear), Orlando Bloom (Romeo and Juliet). Name recognition is now a prerequisite.
- **The musical adaptation loophole.** West Side Story (Romeo and Juliet), Kiss Me Kate (The Taming of the Shrew), Two Gentlemen of Verona (1971 musical) -- Shakespeare increasingly returns to Broadway in musical form.
- **The comedies have migrated downtown.** Twelfth Night, Much Ado, Midsummer, As You Like It are far more common Off-Broadway and in the Park than on Broadway. The tragedies are what still sell tickets uptown.

### Broadway vs. Off-Broadway: Five Key Differences

1. **Scale drives the repertoire.** Broadway revivals cluster around big-cast, big-orchestra spectacles (Porgy and Bess, Show Boat, Carousel, My Fair Lady). Off-Broadway revivals favor intimate, small-cast shows (The Fantasticks, Little Shop, Jacques Brel) because 100-499-seat houses demand it economically.

2. **Off-Broadway has "permanent residents."** The Fantasticks, Stomp, Perfect Crime, Blue Man Group (33 years at Astor Place) lived Off-Broadway for decades with no Broadway ambition. Broadway has no equivalent -- shows there either run or close.

3. **Off-Broadway is Broadway's laboratory.** Assassins (1990 OB to 2004 Broadway), Hedwig (1998 OB to 2014 Broadway), Spring Awakening, Fun Home, Hadestown, Hamilton -- all incubated Off- or Off-Off-Broadway first. The Encores! pipeline is the most systematic version of this.

4. **Shakespeare has essentially migrated downtown.** Broadway averages roughly one Shakespeare production per year now. The Public Theater alone does 2-3 per summer, for free. Add CSC, TFANA, and others, and Off-Broadway produces more Shakespeare in a single season than Broadway does in a decade.

5. **Shows that cross both worlds tend to be more beloved Off-Broadway.** Threepenny Opera's 2,707-performance Off-Broadway run dwarfs its Broadway revivals. Little Shop's current Off-Broadway run (7+ years) far outlasted its Broadway stint (1 year). The intimacy of Off-Broadway often suits these shows better.
