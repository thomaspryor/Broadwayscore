## Why This Show Works

**Wicked** remains one of Broadway's most reliable experiences. The staging is spectacular, the songs are iconic, and the story — a revisionist prequel to *The Wizard of Oz* — still lands emotionally even after two decades.

The current cast brings fresh energy to roles that could easily feel calcified by now. There's a looseness to the performances that suggests genuine ownership rather than carbon-copy replication.

> "Something has changed within me / Something is not the same" — and honestly, every time that song hits, the room shifts.

## What Doesn't Work

The second act still sags in places. The political allegory feels heavier and less nuanced than the first act's character work. And if you're not already invested in Elphaba's journey, the pacing can feel glacial around the midpoint.

The **sound design** continues to be a mixed bag at the Gershwin — the room is just too big for some of the quieter moments to land properly.

## Broadway Stress Test

**Sightlines:** Orchestra center, Row M. Excellent view of the full stage. The dragon over the proscenium is still a great pre-show moment.

**Sound:** Hit or miss. "Defying Gravity" thunders, but some of the dialogue in Act 2 got lost in the cavernous space.

**Intermission:** 15 minutes. The lobby is a zoo — plan ahead if you need anything.

**Audience:** Packed house, heavy tourist crowd. Lots of phones out during curtain call (forgivable). Standing ovation felt genuine.

**Comfort:** Gershwin seats are tight. If you're over 6 feet, splurge for the aisle.
